## 🧠 Pillar 1: The Methodological Mindset

When inspecting a student's scratch paper from this week, we are not looking for simple answers or a chaotic scatter of mental math. We are verifying a highly repetitive, defensive diagnostic routine. True performance under the pressure of an official IB Physics exam relies on the **"Deconstruct-Check-Execute"** loop.

### The Standard Layout Checklist
For every single application problem solved on paper this week, a student's workspace should display three distinct operational zones:
1.  **The Variable Inventory Area:** A neat column on the left listing exactly what data parameters are provided by the examiner, with their corresponding explicit units (e.g., $u_x = 18 \text{ m s}^{-1}$, $t = 20. \text{ s}$) *before* any calculation begins.
2.  **The Unit Filter Gateway:** A visible validation checkpoint checking if those numbers match Base SI metrics. If a student encounters a metric prefix like millivolts ($\text{mV}$) or milliamperes ($\text{mA}$), there must be a literal conversion statement showing $\times 10^{-3}$ written down before hitting a calculator button.
3.  **The Pure Algebraic Anchor:** The target formula must be written out entirely in variable letters first, rearranged using algebraic balancing to isolate the missing variable, and *only then* can the numbers be plugged into the framework. 

*If a student is jumping straight from reading a text prompt to punching numbers into a calculator without setting up an algebraic anchor on paper, they are developing a high-friction habit that mathematically shatters when they hit multi-part kinematics or dynamics derivations in Paper 2.*

## 📋 Pillar 2: The IB Examiner's Lens (Marking Mechanics)

In the IB Diploma Programme, data tracking integrity is prioritized just as heavily as raw physical concept knowledge. Students regularly forfeit substantial chunks of marks because they fail to respect the rigid infrastructure of official mark schemes.

### 1. The "Naked Number" Rule
If an exam prompt asks a student to calculate a net velocity or unified displacement, and they record `2.5` or `81` instead of $2.5 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ or $81 \text{ m}$, the examiner will immediately award **zero marks** for the final conclusion. In the discipline of physics, a numeric value without its defining dimensional unit is completely meaningless.

### 2. Significant Figure Windows
The IB enforces a strict tracking boundary: final answers must align with the precision limits dictated by the starting data, allowing a narrow variance of **plus or minus one significant figure**.
* If the limiting raw data provided by the examiner has 2 significant figures, the mark scheme will only accept a final rounding of 2 sf or 3 sf.
* Writing out an unrounded string from a calculator screen (e.g., `1.333333` instead of $1.3$) triggers an immediate data-tracking penalty and costs an easy mark.

## ⚠️ Pillar 3: The Week 1 Master Minefield (Complete 5-Day Review)

This section maps out the critical conceptual tripwires that historically catch pre-DP students. Review this complete list against your own scratch paper history to diagnose errors.

### 🔍 Days 1 & 2: Base SI Units, Dimensions & Precision Limits
* **The Base vs. Derived Fallacy (Day 1, M2):** Students frequently forget that common units like the Newton ($\text{N}$) or Joule ($\text{J}$) are not fundamental pillars. They are derived compound structures. The Newton must be instinctively recognized as $\text{kg m s}^{-2}$ through the foundational framework of $F=ma$.
* **The Exponent Direction Trap (Day 1, P2):** When isolating the gravitational constant $G$, algebraic errors often crop up when shifting terms across the fraction line. Moving a base unit from a denominator up into a numerator **always flips the algebraic sign of its exponent** ($\frac{1}{\text{s}^2} = \text{s}^{-2}$).
* **Column Alignment vs. Total Counts (Day 2, M4 & P3):** * In **Addition/Subtraction**, precision is strictly limited by the *least number of decimal places*. Adding a massive block ($450 \text{ kg}$, precise only to the tens column) to a tiny mass ($1.25 \text{ kg}$) means the final sum is rounded right back to the tens column: $\color{navy}{450 \text{ kg}}$. The smaller value is swallowed up inside the uncertainty margins of the larger scale.
    * In **Multiplication/Division**, precision is limited by the *least total count of significant figures*.
* **The "Hidden Zero" Trailing Trap (Day 2, M2):** When rounding a calculator output like $4.396$ down to exactly 3 significant figures, the 9 rounds up and forces the number to $4.4$. However, writing `4.4` is marked wrong by examiners because it only displays 2 significant figures. To maintain 3-digit tracking compliance, it *must* be explicitly written as $\color{navy}{4.40}$.

### 🔍 Days 3 & 4: Vectors, Scalars & Component Resolution
* **The Scalar-Vector Divide (Day 3, M2 & P2):** Distance tracks total structural path history ($s = \pi r$ for a half-lap), while Displacement tracks the strict straight-line tracking shortcut from home coordinates to final coordinates ($s = 2r$). Mixing these definitions up will cause a cascading point loss across multi-part kinematic questions.
* **The Spatial Zero Mapping (Day 3, P5):** If a system traces a path through various complex loops but returns exactly to its starting origin point at the end of the day, its net displacement vector collapses instantly to a value of $\color{navy}{0 \text{ m}}$.
* **The Vertical Angle Trap (Day 4, M3):** Standard horizontal coordinate tracking dictates using $A \cos\theta$ for horizontal components and $A \sin\theta$ for vertical components. However, **the moment an examiner measures an angle relative to the vertical y-axis instead of the flat ground, these ratios flip completely!** The horizontal component shifts to a sine assignment ($A \sin\theta_{\text{vertical}}$) and the vertical shifts to a cosine assignment ($A \cos\theta_{\text{vertical}}$). 
* **The Inclined Plane Plane Geometry Pivot (Day 4, P3):** On an inclined ramp surface, gravity resolves differently due to tilted tracking planes. The force pinning the block *into* the ramp is modeled as $W_{\perp} = W \cos\theta$, while the force drawing it *down along* the ramp layout maps to $W_{\parallel} = W \sin\theta$. 

### 🔍 Day 5: Vector Recomposition & Inverse Trigonometry
* **The Calculator Mode Hazard (Day 5, M2 & P2):** When executing inverse tangent operations ($\tan^{-1}$) to deduce heading vectors, students often get bizarre, unrepresentative numbers. This occurs because the hardware defaulted to Radian mode. **Always verify that your physical calculator is locked into Degree mode.**
* **The Missing Parentheses Square (Day 5, M3):** When processing double negative component orientations inside Quadrant 3 ($v_x = -12, v_y = -5.0$), plugging `-12^2` into a calculator without structural grouping will output `-144`, crashing the square root module. Negative tracking components *must* be wrapped in grouping boundaries: $(-12)^2 = +144$.

## 👪 Pillar 4: The Parent Coaching Guide

Parents do not need an academic background in theoretical physics or calculus to audit their child's progress. You can easily evaluate and reinforce their atomic execution habits using three non-technical checking strategies:

1.  **The "Naked Number" Audit:** Pick any completed practice day on your child's screen, look at their hand-written scratch notebook, and look at the final box on their page. If you see numbers sitting by themselves without a trailing SI unit code ($\text{kg}$, $\text{m s}^{-1}$, $\text{J}$, $\text{N}$), they are building an expensive exam habit. Ask them: *"Where are the units on these final markings?"*
2.  **The Clue Dropdown Inspection:** Ask your child to pull up Day 4 or Day 5 on the portal and show you a problem where they had to use a `Reveal Clue` or `Still Stuck` accordion toggle. If they used them, **praise them immediately**. Explain that using a conceptual hint to kickstart independent paper work, closing the window, and struggling through the calculation builds compounding brain power. We want to reward active engineering adjustments over passive solution-copying.
3.  **The Clean Sheet Check:** Look at their scratch notebook layout. Is it structured with legible variable rows and clear lines, or is it a disorganized maze of calculations? A chaotic sheet means a chaotic mind under pressure. Reinforce that a clean tracking page is a student's absolute best defense against simple mathematical mistakes.
