Welcome to Day 1 of your DP Physics Journey! Before we calculate anything, we must master the baseline grammar of the universe: **Units**. In the IB, a number without a unit means absolutely nothing.

---

## Part A: Mathematical Foundations (5 Questions)
*Focus: Mastering algebraic unit mechanics and prefix manipulation.*

### M1. The Base System
In DP Physics, we use the **SI system** (Système International). The three most common base quantities are mass, length, and time. State the standard SI base unit for each of these three quantities.

### M2. Prefix Power
Convert the following laboratory measurements into standard scientific notation meters ($\text{m}$):
* $25.4 \text{ mm}$ (millimeters)
* $0.075 \text{ km}$ (kilometers)
* $480 \mu\text{m}$ (micrometers)

### M3. Deriving the Newt
The Newton ($\text{N}$) is a *derived unit* used for force. Using the classic equation $F = ma$ (Force = mass $\times$ acceleration), break down $1\text{ N}$ completely into its fundamental SI base units ($\text{kg}$, $\text{m}$, $\text{s}$).

### M4. Fractional Exponents & Variables
When dealing with units in denominators, DP Physics avoids using the slash symbol ($/$). Instead, we use negative exponents. Rewrite the following expressions using standard IB notation:
* Meters per second ($\text{m/s}$)
* Kilograms per cubic meter ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
* Joules per kilogram Kelvin ($\text{J / (kg}\cdot\text{K)}$)

### M5. Dimensional Balancing Act
An equation is written as $Z = AB^2$. 
* The unit of $Z$ is $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$.
* The unit of $B$ is $\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$.
Find the fundamental SI base unit of variable $A$ to make this equation dimensionally valid.

## Part B: Physics Applications (5 Questions)
*Focus: Detecting broken formulas and analyzing physical systems.*

### P1. Kinetic Evaluation
The formula for kinetic energy is $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. 
* Show that the units on the right side of the equation resolve to $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$ (which is known as the **Joule**, $\text{J}$).
* Explain why the scalar fraction $\frac{1}{2}$ has no impact on the dimensional correctness of the formula.

### P2. The Broken Pipeline
A student derives an equation for the velocity $v$ of a falling object dropped from height $h$ under gravity $g$ (where $g$ has units of $\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$). Their derived formula is:
$$v = 2gh$$
Prove through dimensional analysis that this student's derived equation is fundamentally broken. State what the correct relationship should be.

### P3. Density Calculations
A container holds a liquid with a density of $\rho = 1.2 \times 10^3 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-3}$. If the volume of the container is $V = 4.0 \times 10^{-2} \text{ m}^3$:
* Deduce the operation (multiplication or division) needed between $\rho$ and $V$ to isolate the mass ($m$) of the liquid.
* Calculate the final mass with its correct unit.

### P4. Period of a Pendulum Preview
The time period $T$ (measured in seconds, $\text{s}$) of a simple swinging pendulum depends on its length $l$ ($\text{m}$) and local gravitational acceleration $g$ ($\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$). 

A student proposes the formula is $T = 2\pi \frac{l}{g}$. Show dimensionally that this formula cannot be correct, and suggest how the variables must be adjusted to fix the time unit.

### P5. The Deep End: Viscous Drag
When a tiny sphere falls through a fluid, it experiences a resistive drag force given by Stokes' Law:
$$F = 6\pi \eta r v$$
* $F$ is force ($\text{N}$)
* $r$ is the radius of the sphere ($\text{m}$)
* $v$ is the velocity of the sphere ($\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$)

The constant $\eta$ represents the viscosity of the fluid. Determine the absolute SI base units for $\eta$.
