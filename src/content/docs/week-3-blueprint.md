Dear Parents,

We have officially completed Week 3! This week marks the structural peak of our mathematical foundations phase. The students moved away from standard numerical arithmetic and entered the world of **symbolic algebraic discipline**.

In MYP science, students are heavily conditioned to treat physics formulas like calculator recipes: they write an equation, immediately plug in numbers, and use basic arithmetic to find a decimal answer. In DP Physics, this habit is fatal. Higher Level exams frequently ask students to derive formulas where no numbers are provided at all. If a student substitutes numbers on line one, they create a messy, rounded decimal trail that masks algebraic patterns and leads to avoidable execution errors.

Our rule this week was strict and absolute: **Symbolic manipulation first, numbers last.** Below is your weekend tracking blueprint.

---

## 🪤 The Week 3 Pitfall Report (What to Check in Their Notebook)

When you look at your child's scratch paper this weekend, check their lines of math for these three critical behavioral traps:

1. **The Line-One Substitution Violation:** Open their notebook to **Day 9** or **Day 10**. Look at the very first line of a problem. Do you see actual physical numbers written down immediately underneath the formula? If numbers appear before line 3, they have violated the core constraint. Force them to restart the problem using only letters until the target variable is completely isolated.
2. **The Perpendicular/Parallel Confusion (Day 11):** Look at their limiting case notes for Friday. When checking an angle boundary, did they write down the fundamental mathematical rules: $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$ and $\cos(0^\circ) = 1$? If they don't know these baseline trigonometric boundaries by heart, they will be unable to run defensive verification checks during exams.
3. **Algebraic Inversion Errors:** In Day 10 (Simultaneous Equations), check to see if they accurately distributed negative signs when substituting multi-term equations. A common mistake is writing something like $60 - (2a + 20)$ as $60 - 2a + 20$, completely forgetting that the negative sign must apply to the 20 as well.

## 🔑 The M/A/N Master Mark Scheme (Days 9–11)

Use this breakdown to cross-verify the structural lines in their scratch notebook.

### 📐 Day 9: Symbolic Formula Manipulation
* **M1 (Isolating Quadratic Time):** * **M (Method):** Set $u = 0 \rightarrow s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$. Multiply by 2, divide by $a$.
    * **A (Accuracy):** $2s = at^2 \rightarrow t^2 = \frac{2s}{a}$
    * **N (Unit/Sig Fig):** $t = \mathbf{\sqrt{\frac{2s}{a}}}$ *(Pure symbolic notation, no units required)*
* **M2 (Extracting from the Denominator):** * **M:** Subtract $\frac{1}{R_2}$ from both sides. Find a common denominator for the right-hand terms.
    * **A:** $\frac{1}{R_1} = \frac{1}{R_p} - \frac{1}{R_2} \rightarrow \frac{1}{R_1} = \frac{R_2 - R_p}{R_p \cdot R_2}$
    * **N:** $R_1 = \mathbf{\frac{R_p \cdot R_2}{R_2 - R_p}}$
* **M3 (Unveiling Kinetic Terms):** * **M/A/N:** Move $2as$ via subtraction, then take the square root of the entire side: $u = \mathbf{\sqrt{v^2 - 2as}}$.
* **M4 (Breaking Out Orbital Radius):** * **M/A/N:** Multiply by $r^2$, divide by $F$, apply the square root: $r = \mathbf{\sqrt{\frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{F}}}$.
* **M5 (Rearranging Exponential Kinetic Energy):** * **M/A/N:** Multiply by 2, divide by $m$, square root: $v = \mathbf{\sqrt{\frac{2E_k}{m}}}$.
* **P1–P5 (Physics Applications Key Results):**
    * **P1 (Centripetal Tension):** Formula: $v = \sqrt{\frac{T \cdot r}{m}}$. Substitution: $\sqrt{\frac{12 \cdot 0.80}{0.15}} = \sqrt{64} = \mathbf{8.0\text{ m s}^{-1}}$ *(2 Sig Figs)*
    * **P2 (Escape Velocity):** Rocket mass $m$ cancels out cleanly from both sides: $v = \mathbf{\sqrt{\frac{2G \cdot M}{R}}}$.
    * **P3 (Braking Distance):** Set $v = 0$ and $a = -d \rightarrow 0 = u^2 - 2ds \rightarrow s = \mathbf{\frac{u^2}{2d}}$.
    * **P4 (Thermal Photon Wavelength):** Multiply by $\lambda$, divide by $E$: $\lambda = \mathbf{\frac{h \cdot c}{E}}$.
    * **P5 (Pendulum Gravity Probe):** Square both sides: $\frac{T^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{L}{g} \rightarrow g = \mathbf{\frac{4\pi^2 \cdot L}{T^2}}$.

### ➕ Day 10: Simultaneous Equations in Physics
* **M1 (Linear Substitution Base):** Isolate $y = 11 - 2x \rightarrow 3x - 2(11 - 2x) = 6 \rightarrow 7x = 28 \rightarrow \mathbf{x = 4}, \mathbf{y = 3}$.
* **M2 (Shared Fractional Unknown):** Cross-multiply Eq 2: $B = 1.5A \rightarrow A + 1.5A = 20 \rightarrow 2.5A = 20 \rightarrow \mathbf{A = 8}, \mathbf{B = 12}$.
* **M3 (Multi-Mass Ratio):** Substitute Eq 1 into Eq 2: $50 - 3a = 5a \rightarrow 50 = 8a \rightarrow \mathbf{a = 6.25}$.
* **M4 (Trigonometric Ratios):** Solve Eq 2 directly: $T_1 = \frac{86.6}{\sin(60^\circ)} = \mathbf{100}$.
* **M5 (Elimination by Addition):** Add equations vertically: $2F = 16 \rightarrow \mathbf{F = 8}, \mathbf{T = 4}$.
* **P1–P5 (Physics Applications Key Results):**
    * **P1 (Connected Train):** $24 - 4.0a = 8.0a \rightarrow 24 = 12.0a \rightarrow \mathbf{a = 2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}}, \mathbf{T = 8.0\text{ N}}$ *(2 Sig Figs)*
    * **P2 (Symmetric Traffic Light):** Eq 1 proves $T_1 = T_2$. Eq 2: $2T_1 \cdot \sin(45^\circ) = 140 \rightarrow \mathbf{T_1 = 99\text{ N}}, \mathbf{T_2 = 99\text{ N}}$.
    * **P3 (Atwood Pulley System):** Isolate $T = 2.0a + 20 \rightarrow 60 - (2.0a + 20) = 6.0a \rightarrow 40 = 8.0a \rightarrow \mathbf{a = 5.0\text{ m s}^{-2}}$.
    * **P4 (Biomechanical Jaw Pivot):** Isolate $F_M = F_J + 15 \rightarrow 2.0(F_J + 15) - 3.0F_J = 0 \rightarrow \mathbf{F_J = 30\text{ N}}, \mathbf{F_M = 45\text{ N}}$.
    * **P5 (Frictionless Elevator):** Substitute $N = 600$ directly: $600 - 500 = 50a \rightarrow 100 = 50a \rightarrow \mathbf{a = 2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}}$.

### 🧗 Day 11: Limiting Case Analysis
* **M1–M5 (Math Foundations Key Validations):**
    * **M1 (Collapsing Fraction):** $\theta=0^\circ \rightarrow a=\mathbf{0}$ (Flat ground, no slide). $\theta=90^\circ \rightarrow a=\mathbf{g}$ (Vertical free fall).
    * **M2 (Incline Press):** $\theta=0^\circ \rightarrow F=\mathbf{m \cdot g}$ (Full weight on floor). $\theta=90^\circ \rightarrow F=\mathbf{0}$ (No wall pressure).
    * **M3 (Infinite Denominator):** As $m \rightarrow \infty$, $v = \frac{k}{\infty} \rightarrow \mathbf{0}$ (Infinite inertia cannot move).
    * **M4 (Atwood Pulley Shift):** Set $m_1 = 0 \rightarrow a = \frac{m_2 \cdot g}{m_2} = \mathbf{g}$ (Pure free fall without resistance).
    * **M5 (Algebraic Red Flag):** $\theta=0^\circ \rightarrow \sin(0^\circ)=0 \rightarrow T = \frac{mg}{0} = \mathbf{\infty}$. True infinity reveals an inverted derivation.
* **P1–P5 (Physics Applications Insight Selection):**
    * **P1 (Velocity Layout):** **Form A** provides insight because placing mass in the denominator visually clarifies the inverse square root relationship.
    * **P2 (Conveyor Belt Error):** Faulty equation claims $N = 0$ on flat ground ($\theta = 0^\circ$), which is physically impossible. Swapped $\sin$/$\cos$.
    * **P3 (Probe Radius Limit):** As $r \rightarrow \infty$, $v \rightarrow \mathbf{0}$ (Zero orbital speed needed at infinite distance due to zero gravity).
    * **P4 (Wave Mechanics Layout):** **Form A** is preferred because it splits structural drivers (tension) from inertial resistors (density).
    * **P5 (Banking Extremes):** Flat turn ($\theta = 0^\circ$) yields $v = \mathbf{0}$. Correct: with zero friction on flat ground, any speed results in a skid.

## 👪 The 60-Second Parent Accountability Check-In

You do not need to understand complex algebra to verify your child's conceptual baseline this weekend. Open their notebook or a blank sheet of paper and ask this exact verbal coaching question:

💬 **"Imagine a heavy hanging weight is suspended from the ceiling by a single string. What happens to the total tension force inside that string if the angle it makes with the ceiling opens up and approaches exactly 90 degrees (hanging straight down)?"**

### What to look for in their response:
* **An Elite Response:** They can explain that as the string tilts closer to 90 degrees (straight vertical alignment), it becomes perfectly optimized to support the weight. The required tension drops to its minimum possible value, which is exactly equal to the object's true weight ($W = mg$). 
* **A "Red Flag" Response:** They guess wildly, say tension goes to infinity, or try to memorize a random formula without being able to explain the physical layout. If they struggle, remind them that testing limits ($\theta = 90^\circ$ versus $\theta = 0^\circ$) is their automatic defense shield against structural math errors.
