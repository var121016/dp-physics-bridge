Dear Parents,

We have officially entered **Phase 2: Core Physics Launch**. Week 3 focused on the "language" of physics (algebra), and now we are using that language to describe the "world" of physics (motion). This week marks a shift from 1D motion (lines) to 2D dynamics (geometry). 

In MYP science, students are often taught to view inclined planes as "magic" triangles where they just guess whether to use $\sin$ or $\cos$. In DP Physics, this is a recipe for failure. Higher Level exams present complex, rotated systems where simple guessing is impossible. Our rule this week: **Always rotate the axis to the slope.** If the object is on a ramp, the ramp is the new "flat." Below is your weekend tracking blueprint.

---

### 🪤 The Week 4 Pitfall Report (What to Check in Their Notebook)
When you look at your child’s scratch paper this weekend, check their work for these three critical behavioral traps:

1. **The "Lazy Axis" Violation:** Look at their work for **Day 14**. Do you see FBDs where the axes are standard horizontal/vertical even though the object is on a ramp? If they haven't drawn a tilted $x/y$ coordinate system, they will inevitably miscalculate the weight components. They must show the axis rotation.
2. **Gravity Decomposition Errors:** On an incline, gravity does not point along the $y$-axis. Check their work: they must explicitly write $W_\parallel = mg \sin\theta$ and $W_\perp = mg \cos\theta$. If they are swapping $\sin$ and $\cos$, they aren't visualizing the geometry.
3. **The "Forgotten Component" Trap:** In **Day 13**, look for $\Sigma F = ma$ equations. If they only include the applied force and ignore the Normal force or Friction, they are still thinking in "linear" terms. A system must balance *perpendicular* to the motion, even if it is accelerating *parallel* to it.

### 🔑 The M/A/N Master Mark Scheme (Days 12–14)
Use this breakdown to cross-verify the structural lines in their scratch notebook.

#### 📐 Day 12: Laws as Equations
* **M1 (Force Balance):** $\Sigma F = ma \rightarrow F - f = ma$. Isolate $a = (F-f)/m$.
* **M2 (Weight & Mass):** $W = mg$. Ensure they converted mass to kg if provided in grams.
* **M3 (Newton's 3rd):** Equal and opposite. $F_{AB} = -F_{BA}$.
* **M4 (Dynamic Friction):** $f_k = \mu_k N$. Check for $N = mg$ (only true on flat ground).
* **M5 (Net Force):** $\Sigma F = F_{app} - f_k$. Correct sign usage is mandatory.

#### 📐 Day 13: 2D & Inclines (The Rotation Rules)
* **M1 (Resolution):** $F_x = F \cos\theta, F_y = F \sin\theta$.
* **M2 (Incline Gravity):** $W_\parallel = mg \sin\theta, W_\perp = mg \cos\theta$.
* **M3 (Resultants):** $R = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2}$. Check for Pythagoras.
* **M4 (Normal on Incline):** $N = W_\perp = mg \cos\theta$.
* **M5 (Angle Discovery):** $\theta = \arccos(F_x/F)$.

#### 📐 Day 14: Friction Dynamics
* **M1 (Static Limit):** $f_s \leq \mu_s N$. Verify they didn't treat static friction as a constant value.
* **M2 (Kinetic Friction):** $f_k = \mu_k N$.
* **M3 (Incline Friction):** $f_s = mg \sin\theta$.
* **M4 (Coeff of Friction):** $\mu = f/N$.
* **M5 (Max Applied):** $F_{max} = \mu_s N + f_{applied}$.

### 👪 The 60-Second Parent Accountability Check-In
You don't need to be a physics expert to verify their conceptual baseline. Open their notebook and ask this exact verbal coaching question:

💬 **“Imagine a box is sitting on a ramp, but the ramp is perfectly frictionless. If you increase the steepness of that ramp, does the Normal force (the force pushing the box into the ramp) get larger or smaller? Why?”**

**What to look for in their response:**
* **An Elite Response:** They explain that as the angle $\theta$ increases toward 90 degrees, $\cos\theta$ decreases. Therefore, the Normal force gets **smaller**. At 90 degrees, the box is in free-fall and the Normal force becomes zero.
* **A "Red Flag" Response:** They guess "larger" or "it stays the same." If they guess, ask them: *"What happens to the Normal force if you turn the ramp into a vertical wall?"* Their realization that a vertical wall exerts zero Normal force on a falling object will help them self-correct.
