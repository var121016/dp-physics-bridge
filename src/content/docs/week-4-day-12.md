Welcome to Day 12! Today we launch **Phase 2: Mechanics Core**. We begin by mastering the single most critical translation skill in physics: taking a complex real-world word problem and turning it into a clean, geometric Free Body Diagram (FBD).

### ⏱️ The 15-Minute Execution Rule
* An FBD represents forces acting **ON** the chosen object, never the forces the object exerts on its surroundings.
* Every force vector must originate as a clean arrow pointing *outward* from a centralized point mass dot.
* You must categorize every arrow as either a **Field Force** or a **Contact Force**.

### M2. Isolating the Target Object Point Mass
A wooden crate rests on the flat bed of a delivery truck. The truck is actively accelerating forward. A student draws a vector arrow representing the engine's drive force directly onto the crate's FBD. Explain why this is a severe structural error.

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** An FBD must *only* contain forces that interact directly with the boundary of your specific isolated object. Is the truck engine physically touching or directly pulling the wooden crate?

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

**The engine's drive force belongs on the truck's FBD, never the crate's FBD.**

*Physics Justification:* The engine drives the truck tires forward. The crate does not know the engine exists; it moves forward solely because of the **Static Friction Force ($f_s$)** acting on its bottom surface from the truck bed. Including the engine force violates the rule that an FBD contains only forces acting *directly* on the chosen body.
</details>

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

The upward tension arrow ($T$) must be drawn exactly **three times longer** than the downward weight arrow ($W$). Because $150\text{ N} / 50\text{ N} = 3$, failure to draw a visibly asymmetric upward line will cost you the accuracy mark on Paper 2.

<details>

## 🚀 Part 2: Physics Applications

### P1. The Passenger in a Rising Lift
Draw and describe the complete FBD for a passenger standing inside an elevator car that is actively accelerating **straight upwards**. Identify all forces and state which vector arrow must be drawn longer.

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** The passenger is touching the floor of the lift. If they are accelerating upwards, what does Newton's Second Law tell us about the balance of these forces?

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

The FBD contains exactly two vertical forces acting on the passenger's center dot:
1. **Normal Force ($N$):** Pointing straight upwards (exerted by the floor of the lift on the passenger's feet).
2. **Weight ($W$):** Pointing straight downwards.

*Scale Rule:* Because the lift is accelerating upwards, there must be a net upward force ($\Sigma F > 0$). Therefore, the **upward Normal Force arrow must be drawn noticeably longer** than the downward Weight arrow.
</details>

### P2. The Coasting Skateboarder
A child on a skateboard rolls along a flat concrete pavement. They have stopped kicking, and are slowly coasting to a stop due to a small friction force. Draw and label the active FBD for the child-skateboard system.

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** Even though the child is moving forward, is there any active physical contact pushing them forward? Map out your vertical balance and your horizontal retarding force carefully.

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

The FBD contains exactly three forces:
1. **Weight ($W$):** Pointing straight downwards.
2. **Normal Force ($N$):** Pointing straight upwards (exactly equal in length to $W$, since there is no vertical acceleration).
3. **Friction ($f$):** Pointing **horizontally backwards** (opposing the coasting direction).

*Critical Trap:* There is **no forward arrow**. A common error is drawing a "motion force" arrow pointing forward. Inertia keeps the skateboard moving forward; forces only exist due to direct physical or field interactions!
</details>

### P3. The Hanging Chandelier Rig
A heavy decorative chandelier of mass $m$ hangs completely stationary from a high ballroom ceiling by a vertical steel wire. Draw the FBD, state the name of the upward force, and write down the net force value ($\Sigma F$).

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** The system is in a state of absolute static equilibrium. What does the word "stationary" mean for acceleration, and therefore for net force?

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

The FBD consists of two perfectly equal and opposite vector lines:
1. **Tension Force ($T$):** Pointing straight upwards along the path of the wire.
2. **Weight ($W$):** Pointing straight downwards.

*Net Force:* Because the chandelier is stationary, its acceleration is zero. Therefore, the net force is exactly **$\Sigma F = 0$**, meaning $T = W$.
</details>

### P4. The Slanted Book on an Incline
A textbook sits completely at rest on a desk that has been tilted up at an angle of $20^\circ$. Draw the complete FBD for the textbook, ensuring you show the correct angular alignment for all surface contact forces.

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** Gravity always pulls straight down toward the center of the Earth, regardless of surface tilt. The normal force must remain perpendicular to the desk surface, and friction acts parallel to prevent the book from sliding down.

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

The FBD must show three distinct arrows originating from the textbook dot:
1. **Weight ($W$):** Pointing **straight down** toward the bottom edge of your page.
2. **Normal Force ($N$):** Pointing at an angle perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the slanted surface of the desk.
3. **Static Friction ($f_s$):** Pointing straight **up the slant of the desk** (parallel to the surface, preventing the book from sliding down).
</details>

### P5. The Weather Balloon in Steady Ascent
A meteorological weather balloon filled with helium rises vertically through the atmosphere at a **perfectly constant velocity**. Draw the FBD for the balloon housing.

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

**💡 Reveal Clue:** "Constant velocity" is the single most important wording clue in DP Physics. It means acceleration is zero. Therefore, your upward forces must perfectly balance your downward forces. What pushes a balloon up?

---

**👀 Visual Reference Layout:** 🔗 [View Full FBD Vector Layout](/week-4-day-12-fbd-assets#m1-fbd-layout)

The FBD consists of:
1. **Buoyant Force ($F_B$):** Pointing straight upwards (caused by the displacement of surrounding air molecules).
2. **Weight ($W$):** Pointing straight downwards.
3. **Air Resistance ($F_D$):** Pointing **straight downwards** (since the balloon is moving upwards, drag opposes its motion).

*Balance Equation:* The upward arrow length ($F_B$) must exactly equal the combined total lengths of the two downward arrows ($W + F_D$) so that $\Sigma F = 0$.
</details>
