Welcome to Day 2! Today we focus on precision. In physics, the numbers we use come from real-world measurements. The number of digits we record tells us exactly how precise our measuring tools were. 

---

## Part A: Mathematical Foundations (5 Questions)
*Focus: Counting significant figures and converting to standard scientific notation.*

### M1. The Counting Rules
Determine the number of significant figures (sf) in each of the following values:
* $0.00340 \text{ kg}$
* $1200 \text{ N}$
* $1.005 \times 10^{4} \text{ s}$
* $400. \text{ m}$ (note the decimal point)

### M2. Taming the Calculator Output
A calculator outputs the raw result $x = 4.396152$. Round this number to:
* 1 significant figure
* 2 significant figures
* 3 significant figures
* 4 significant figures

### M3. Standard Form Conversion
Convert the following large and small numbers into standard scientific notation ($A \times 10^n$, where $1 \le A < 10$) while maintaining their original precision:
* $150,000,000,000 \text{ m}$ (Distance from Earth to Sun)
* $0.0000000000000000001602 \text{ C}$ (Charge of an electron)
* $6,670,000 \text{ kg}$

### M4. Adding and Subtracting Rules
For adding and subtracting, the rule is based on the **least number of decimal places**. Calculate the final result using the correct precision:
$$12.55 \text{ g} + 3.2 \text{ g} - 0.004 \text{ g}$$

### M5. Multiplying and Dividing Rules
For multiplying and dividing, the rule is based on the **least number of significant figures**. Calculate the final result using the correct precision:
$$\frac{450.3 \text{ m}}{1.5 \text{ s}}$$

## Part B: Physics Applications (5 Questions)
*Focus: Applying data tracking and precision adjustments to physical scenarios.*

### P1. Speed Trap Precision
A student uses a stopwatch to measure the time it takes a toy car to travel a fixed distance. 
* The distance is measured to be $d = 2.40 \text{ m}$ (3 sf).
* The time is recorded as $t = 1.8 \text{ s}$ (2 sf).

Calculate the average speed ($v = \frac{d}{t}$) of the car. Express your final answer with the mathematically correct number of significant figures and its proper IB unit.

### P2. Area of a Solar Panel
An engineer is designing a rectangular solar panel for a satellite. The length of the panel is measured as $3.15 \text{ m}$ and the width is measured as $0.42 \text{ m}$. 
* Calculate the surface area of the panel.
* State why it would be physically incorrect to report the answer as $1.323 \text{ m}^2$.

### P3. The Mass of a Compound Block
Two metallic blocks are welded together. Block A has a mass of $m_A = 4.5 \times 10^2 \text{ kg}$ and Block B has a mass of $m_B = 1.25 \text{ kg}$. 
* Convert both masses out of scientific notation into standard numbers.
* Calculate the total mass ($m_A + m_B$) using correct significant figure constraints. Explain why Block B's precision is effectively "swallowed up" by Block A.

### P4. Electric Current Scale
An electric circuit has a current of $I = 4.5 \text{ mA}$ (milliamperes) flowing through it for a time of $t = 2.0 \times 10^2 \text{ s}$. The formula for total charge delivered is:
$$Q = I \times t$$
Calculate the total charge $Q$ in Coulombs ($\text{C}$), ensuring that your prefixes are converted to base SI units and your final answer reflects the correct number of significant figures.

### P5. Gravitational Force Scale Check
The universal gravitational force formula between two objects is given by:
$$F = \frac{G \cdot M \cdot m}{r^2}$$
If a student plugs in values and their calculator spits out $F = 345.28911 \text{ N}$, but the mass with the poorest precision in the data set was given to only **3 significant figures**, state exactly how the student must write this final value on an IB exam paper.
