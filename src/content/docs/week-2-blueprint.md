## 🪤 The Week 2 Pitfall Report (What to Check in Their Notebook)

When you look at your child's scratch paper this weekend, look closely at their sketches for these three high-friction mistakes:

1. **The Missing Direction Anchor:** In Day 7 (Vector Addition), did they draw an explicit coordinate cross (e.g., an arrow pointing up with a plus sign, or pointing right with a plus sign) at the top of the problem? If they didn't anchor their signs, they are highly likely to guess whether numbers should be positive or negative.
2. **The Incline Plane Trap (Day 8):** Look at their triangles for the ramp problems. MYP students instinctively think that "horizontal is always cosine ($\cos$) and vertical is always sine ($\sin$)." On a ramp, this flips. The force pulling *down* the ramp uses $\sin$, and the force pushing *into* the ramp uses $\cos$. Check to see if they caught this flip or fell into the trap.
3. **Calculator Mode Errors:** If your child understands the physics perfectly but keeps getting wildly incorrect numbers, check their actual physical calculator screen. It must say **DEG** (Degrees), not **RAD** (Radians). Running vector triangles in Radian mode is one of the most common ways students lose marks early on.

## 🔑 The M/A/N Master Mark Scheme (Days 6–8)

Use this breakdown to cross-verify the execution steps in their scratch notebook.

### 📐 Day 6: SOH CAH TOA & Component Resolution
* **M1 (The Baseline Split):** * **M (Method):** $A_x = A \cdot \cos(\theta)$, $A_y = A \cdot \sin(\theta)$
    * **A (Accuracy):** $A_x = 12.0 \cdot \cos(30^\circ) = 10.392$, $A_y = 12.0 \cdot \sin(30^\circ) = 6.000$
    * **N (Unit/Sig Fig):** $A_x = \mathbf{10.4\text{ m}}$, $A_y = \mathbf{6.00\text{ m}}$ *(Strict 3 Sig Figs)*
* **M2 (Quadrant 2 Navigation):** * **M:** West is negative x, North is positive y. $v_x = -v \cdot \cos(\theta)$
    * **A:** $v_x = -45 \cdot \cos(40^\circ) = -34.47$
    * **N:** $v_x = \mathbf{-34\text{ m s}^{-1}}$, $v_y = \mathbf{+29\text{ m s}^{-1}}$ *(Rounded strictly to 2 Sig Figs due to the '45')*
* **M3 (Reconstructing the Resultant):** * **M:** $s = \sqrt{s_x^2 + s_y^2}$, $\phi = \tan^{-1}(|s_y| / s_x)$
    * **A:** $s = \sqrt{8.00^2 + (-6.00)^2} = 10.00$, $\phi = \tan^{-1}(6/8) = 36.86^\circ$
    * **N:** $s = \mathbf{10.0\text{ m}}$, $\theta = \mathbf{36.9^\circ\text{ below the positive x-axis}}$
* **M4 (Double-Negative Recomposition):** * **M:** $F = \sqrt{(-12.0)^2 + (-5.00)^2}$ *(Must include parentheses to cancel negatives)*
    * **A:** $F = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169}$
    * **N:** $F = \mathbf{13.0\text{ N}}$
* **M5 (Pure Axial Alignment):** * **M/A/N:** Concept-only problem. Vector points straight down. $A_x = \mathbf{0\text{ m}}$, $A_y = \mathbf{-255\text{ m}}$.
* **P1–P5 (Physics Applications Key Results):**
    * **P1 (Cargo Sled):** $320 \cdot \cos(25^\circ) = \mathbf{290\text{ N}}$ *(2 Sig Figs)*
    * **P2 (Weather Balloon):** $14.5 \cdot \sin(70.0^\circ) = \mathbf{13.6\text{ m s}^{-1}}$ *(3 Sig Figs)*
    * **P3 (Runway Blast-Off):** $\sqrt{75.0^2 - 72.2^2} = \mathbf{20.3\text{ m s}^{-1}}$
    * **P4 (Drone Delivery):** $\sqrt{40.0^2 + (-30.0)^2} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ m}}$
    * **P5 (Soccer Kick):** $\tan^{-1}(12/16) = \mathbf{37^\circ}$ *(2 Sig Figs)*

## 👪 The 60-Second Parent Accountability Check-In

You do not need to know any physics or mathematics to verify your child's progress. This weekend, open their scratch notebook to **Day 8 (Inclined Planes)**, point your finger at one of their ramp diagrams, and ask this exact question:

💬 **"I see you drew a right triangle inside this ramp diagram. Can you point to the arrow that represents gravity pulling straight down, and show me why the angle at the top of that small triangle matches the angle of the ramp?"**

### What to look for in their response:
* **An Elite Response:** They can confidently point to the vertical arrow pointing straight toward the bottom of the page (true weight) and explain that as the ramp tilts up, the angle between gravity and the perpendicular axis opens up by the exact same amount. They show you that they didn't just copy a formula; they understand the geometric tilt.
* **A "Red Flag" Response:** They shrug, look confused, or say, *"I don't know, the clue online just told me to multiply by sine, so I did."* If they say this, remind them that the DP exam will change the reference angle layout to trick them. Encourage them to re-read the Day 8 introduction and re-trace the triangle manually.
