Today we unlock the primary algebraic stabilizer of trigonometry. Just as the Pythagorean theorem coordinates the straight boundary lengths of a right-angled triangle, its trigonometric equivalent structures the relationship between sine and cosine values across any angle. We will master how to manipulate this fundamental identity to simplify complex rational expressions and substitute parametric components cleanly.

---

## 🧭 The Core Strategy Blueprint

### 👥 Parent Analytics Summary
* **The Core Gap:** Students often treat $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ as an isolated fact, failing to recognize its alternative algebraic arrangements. When confronted with blocks like $1 - \sin^2\theta$ or $\sin^2\theta - 1$, they fail to see them as simple cosine substitutions, which leads to bloated, dead-end algebraic tracking.
* **The Target Objective:** We derive the identity directly from unit circle coordinates using first principles. Your student will learn to instinctively recognize all alternative forms of the identity, integrate it with Week 1 factoring strategies (like the difference of squares), and collapse complex trigonometric fractions with structural confidence.

### 🎒 First-Principles Script (The Feynman Technique)
> **Read this aloud to lock in the structural intuition:**
>
> "Let's discover why the most famous identity in trigonometry is actually just the Pythagorean theorem in a clever disguise. 
> 
> Yesterday, we proved that if you draw a right-angled triangle inside a unit circle of radius 1, the horizontal adjacent side length matches your $x$-coordinate ($\cos\theta$), and the vertical opposite side length matches your $y$-coordinate ($\sin\theta$). The hypotenuse is always the radius of the circle, which is exactly **1**.
> 
> Let's apply the classic Pythagorean theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$) straight to this coordinate triangle:
> 
> $$(\text{Adjacent})^2 + (\text{Opposite})^2 = (\text{Hypotenuse})^2 \implies (x)^2 + (y)^2 = (1)^2$$
> 
> Substituting our trigonometric coordinate engines unmasks our fundamental identity:
> 
> $$\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$$
> 
> This identity is an absolute algebraic powerhouse because it allows us to convert sine completely into cosine, and vice versa. To exploit its full power, you must master its two alternative rearranged profiles:
> * **Isolating Cosine:** $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta$
> * **Isolating Sine:** $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$
> 
> Look closely at the right-hand side of those setups. Expressions like $1 - \sin^2\theta$ match the algebraic layout of a **Difference of Squares** ($1^2 - u^2$). That means you can instantly factorize them into two binomial brackets whenever you need to cancel out a tricky denominator fraction:
> 
> $1 - \sin^2\theta = (1 - \sin\theta)(1 + \sin\theta)$
> 
> Keep your eyes sharp for these patterns—they are the key to collapsing complex trigonometric problems instantly."

#### 10. Problem 10: The Elimination Invariant of Bivariate Parametric Systems
Let us execute a complete algebraic validation exploring how the fundamental identity can completely untangle complex, multi-variable parametric equations. Suppose we have a system tracking a particle's position coordinates across space over time ($\theta$), where the tracking paths are governed by two interrelated parameters, $A$ and $B$:

$$x = A\cos(\theta) + B\sin(\theta) \quad \text{and} \quad y = A\sin(\theta) - B\cos(\theta)$$

Where coefficients $A, B \in \mathbb{R}^+$ represent fixed scalar scales, and angle variable $\theta$ changes continuously across all real domains.

* **Part A:** Calculate the explicit polynomial expansion line for the squared horizontal position metric $x^2$. Do not attempt any identity replacements yet.
* **Part B:** Calculate the explicit polynomial expansion line for the squared vertical position metric $y^2$. Maintain all distributed coefficient structures cleanly.
* **Part C:** **Prove that** if we combine these squared coordinate metrics through addition ($x^2 + y^2$), the entire angular variable element $\theta$ cancels out of the engine completely. Show that the combined total simplifies to a single invariant constant statement that depends only on the system's baseline coefficients:

$$x^2 + y^2 = A^2 + B^2$$

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>

**Structural Connection:** This advanced challenge combines **Week 6 Pythagorean identity rules** with **Week 1 multi-variable expansion and factoring loops**.

For Parts A and B, use the perfect square trinomial expansion identity: $(u \pm v)^2 = u^2 \pm 2uv + v^2$. Be careful to apply the exponents to the coefficients ($A^2, B^2$) as well as the functions. For Part C, add the equations together vertically. Notice that the complex cross-product terms ($2AB\sin\theta\cos\theta$) have opposite signs and cancel completely, allowing you to factor out $A^2$ and $B^2$ and apply the fundamental identity.
</details>

<details>
<summary>📋 Expand Full Mark Scheme</summary>

* **Step 1 [Part A]:** Expand the horizontal position squared binomial expression completely using perfect square laws:
  $$x^2 = (A\cos\theta + B\sin\theta)^2 = A^2\cos^2\theta + 2AB\cos\theta\sin\theta + B^2\sin^2\theta$$ **[1A]**
* **Step 2 [Part B]:** Expand the vertical position squared binomial expression completely using perfect square laws:
  $$y^2 = (A\sin\theta - B\cos\theta)^2 = A^2\sin^2\theta - 2AB\sin\theta\cos\theta + B^2\cos^2\theta$$ **[1A]**
* **Step 3 [Part C]:** Set up the addition tracking structure for $x^2 + y^2$:
  $$x^2 + y^2 = \left[A^2\cos^2\theta + 2AB\cos\theta\sin\theta + B^2\sin^2\theta\right] + \left[A^2\sin^2\theta - 2AB\sin\theta\cos\theta + B^2\cos^2\theta\right]$$ **[1M]**
* **Step 4 [Part C]:** Eliminate the cross-product terms: The $+2AB\cos\theta\sin\theta$ and $-2AB\sin\theta\cos\theta$ terms are additive inverses and cancel out to exactly zero. **[1M]**
* **Step 5 [Part C]:** Regroup the remaining components by grouping common constant coefficient terms together:
  $$x^2 + y^2 = \left(A^2\cos^2\theta + A^2\sin^2\theta\right) + \left(B^2\sin^2\theta + B^2\cos^2\theta\right)$$ **[1M]**
* **Step 6 [Part C]:** Factor out coefficient $A^2$ from the first block and coefficient $B^2$ from the second block:
  $$x^2 + y^2 = A^2\left(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta\right) + B^2\left(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta\right)$$ **[1M]**
* **Step 7 [Part C]:** Substitute the fundamental identity ($\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$) into both bracket sections:
  $$x^2 + y^2 = A^2(1) + B^2(1)$$ **[1M]**
* **Step 8 [Part C]:** Simplify to state the final invariant identity expression line:
  $$x^2 + y^2 = A^2 + B^2$$
  Because the trigonometric terms collapse to 1, the variable angle parameter $\theta$ is completely eliminated from the calculation engine, completing the structural proof. **[1A]** *(Identity verified completely—Q.E.D.)*
