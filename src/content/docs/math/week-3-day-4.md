## 🧭 The Core Strategy Blueprint

### 👥 Parent Analytics Summary
* **The Core Gap:** In middle school courses, students are conditioned to use $y = mx + c$. They substitute a point to calculate $c$, rewrite the equation, and rearrange it. This multi-step process introduces frequent arithmetic errors. More importantly, when students encounter IB DP Calculus or Vector geometry, the components are hidden behind variables (e.g., tracking a tangent through $(x_0, f(x_0))$). If they rely on $y=mx+c$, the algebra grinds to a halt.
* **The Target Objective:** We are hardwiring **Point-Slope form**: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$. This structural tool creates the entire equation in exactly one step. It isolates the structural anchoring point $(x_1, y_1)$ cleanly from the directional slope parameter $m$, aligning perfectly with the advanced functional mechanics studied in **Week 3, Days 1 and 2**.

### 🎒 First-Principles Script (The Feynman Technique)
> **Read this aloud to lock in the structural intuition:**
>
> "Let's strip the equation of a straight line back down to its absolute birth state. 
>
> What is the definition of a gradient ($m$)? It is simply the vertical change divided by the horizontal change between any two points on a line. If we pick a fixed anchoring point $(x_1, y_1)$ and a completely random moving point anywhere else on that same line $(x, y)$, our gradient formula states:
>
> $$m = \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1}$$
>
> Now, perform a single algebraic cross-multiplication. Multiply both sides by the denominator $(x - x_1)$. What do you get?
>
> $$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$
>
> This is Point-Slope form. It is not a new recipe to memorize; it is literally just the definition of a gradient rewritten on a single line. 
> 
> Look at its structural beauty: if an examiner hands you a slope of $3$ and a point $(4, -2)$, you don't calculate anything. You inject them directly into their slots: $y - (-2) = 3(x - 4) \implies y + 2 = 3(x - 4)$. Done. You have instantly locked down the entire line equation in one clean motion, bypassing the hunt for a $y$-intercept entirely."

#### 10. Problem 10: The Dynamic Tangent Intercept Optimization Locus
