## 💡 Core Strategy Blueprint
1. **Negative Powers:** Flip the base framework to make the index positive: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ or $\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^n$.
2. **Fractional Powers:** Denominator is the radical root, numerator is the exponent power: $a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$.
3. **Conjugate Rationalization:** Multiply $(a \pm \sqrt{b})$ by $(a \mp \sqrt{b})$ to execute the difference of squares: $(a-b)(a+b) = a^2 - b^2$.

### 🔄 Tier 2: The Multi-Step Twist

#### Problem 5
Solve for $x$:  
$$2^{x+2} + 2^x = 40$$

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>
Deconstruct $2^{x+2}$ into $2^x \times 2^2$ using exponent sum laws, then factor out the common term $2^x$.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🔑 Expand Full Markscheme</summary>

* **Step 1:** Separate the expanded index tracking parameter:  
  $$2^x(2^2) + 2^x = 40$$
* **Step 2:** Factor out the baseline element:  
  $$2^x(2^2 + 1) = 40 \implies 2^x(5) = 40$$
* **Step 3:** Isolate and solve the resulting logarithmic base:  
  $$2^x = 8 \implies 2^x = 2^3 \implies x = 3$$

**Final Answer Bounds:** $x = 3$
</details>

#### Problem 7
Solve the simultaneous equations:  
$$\begin{cases} y = 2x - 1 \\ x^2 + y^2 = 13 \end{cases}$$

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>
Substitute the linear equation for $y$ directly into the second quadratic system equation, expand the brackets carefully, and factor the resulting quadratic trinomial.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🔑 Expand Full Markscheme</summary>

* **Step 1:** Execute variable substitution path:  
  $$x^2 + (2x - 1)^2 = 13$$
* **Step 2:** Expand and move all integers to the left side:  
  $$x^2 + 4x^2 - 4x + 1 = 13 \implies 5x^2 - 4x - 12 = 0$$
* **Step 3:** Factor the quadratic expression:  
  $$(5x + 6)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 2 \text{ or } x = -\frac{6}{5}$$
* **Step 4:** Back-substitute to find corresponding $y$-coordinates:  
  * If $x = 2$: $y = 2(2) - 1 = 3$  
  * If $x = -\frac{6}{5}$: $y = 2\left(-\frac{6}{5}\right) - 1 = -\frac{17}{5}$

**Final Answer Bounds:** $(2, 3)$ and $\left(-\frac{6}{5}, -\frac{17}{5}\right)$
</details>

### 🔍 Tier 3: The "Show That..."

#### Problem 8
Show that the expression $\frac{3^{n+2} - 3^n}{3^{n+1} + 3^n}$ simplifies to exactly $2$ for all real values of $n$.

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>
Factor out the lowest common exponent power, $3^n$, from every term in both the numerator and the denominator.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🔑 Expand Full Markscheme</summary>

* **Step 1:** Factor out $3^n$ separately from both the top and bottom layers:  
  $$\frac{3^n(3^2 - 1)}{3^n(3^1 + 1)}$$
* **Step 2:** Cancel the matching exponential term $3^n$:  
  $$\frac{3^2 - 1}{3^1 + 1}$$
* **Step 3:** Evaluate the remaining basic integer constants:  
  $$\frac{9 - 1}{3 + 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$

**The statement has been shown completely.**
</details>

#### Problem 9
Show that $\left(\sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 - \left(\sqrt{x} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2$ simplifies to a constant integer, and state its value.

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>
Expand both sets of brackets using standard algebraic patterns: $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ and $(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🔑 Expand Full Markscheme</summary>

* **Step 1:** Expand the left-hand squared bracket:  
  $$\left(\sqrt{x} + \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 = x + 2\left(\sqrt{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right) + \frac{1}{x} = x + 2 + \frac{1}{x}$$
* **Step 2:** Expand the right-hand squared bracket:  
  $$\left(\sqrt{x} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right)^2 = x - 2\left(\sqrt{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\right) + \frac{1}{x} = x - 2 + \frac{1}{x}$$
* **Step 3:** Subtract the expanded tracking expressions using sign distributions:  
  $$\left(x + 2 + \frac{1}{x}\right) - \left(x - 2 + \frac{1}{x}\right) = x - x + 2 - (-2) + \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x} = 4$$

**Final Answer Bounds:** $4$
</details>

### 📊 Tier 4: Junior Paper 3 Investigation

#### Problem 10: The Nested Radical Investigation
Consider the infinite repeating nested square root:  
$$X = \sqrt{6 + \sqrt{6 + \sqrt{6 + \dots}}}$$

**(a)** Square both sides of the equation to express $X^2$ in terms of $X$.  
**(b)** Rearrange this into a standard quadratic equation.  
**(c)** Solve the quadratic equation for $X$.  
**(d)** Explain briefly why one of your solutions must be rejected, and state the final true value of $X$.

<details>
<summary>💡 View Strategy Hint</summary>
Notice that because the radical extends to infinity, the nested sequence underneath the main outer square root is identical to the original definition of $X$.
</details>

<details>
<summary>🔑 Expand Full Markscheme</summary>

* **(a)** Squaring both sides drops the outer radical layer. Because the sequence underneath repeats to infinity, it evaluates directly to $X$:  
  $$X^2 = 6 + \sqrt{6 + \sqrt{6 + \dots}} \implies X^2 = 6 + X$$
* **(b)** Balance all spatial components to the left side to match a standard quadratic form:  
  $$X^2 - X - 6 = 0$$
* **(c)** Execute trinomial factoring matrix options:  
  $$(X - 3)(X + 2) = 0 \implies X = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad X = -2$$
* **(d)** The principal radical definition requires that $X > 0$, as a square root operation cannot return a negative value. Therefore, $X = -2$ must be rejected.

**Final Answer Bounds:** $X = 3$
</details>
